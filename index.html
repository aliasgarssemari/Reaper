It doesn't ask "does this look like them?"

It asks "how far apart are these two points in identity space?" A face becomes a coordinate — and the same person stays nearby across age, lighting and camera changes that would fool a human eye.

Reliability is engineered, not hoped for.

One frame is a rumour. Iris Mode demands consensus: embeddings fused across good frames, flip-TTA mirror averaging, quality gating that discards bad poses rather than letting them poison the vote.

The margin rule kills look-alikes.

A match must clear the acceptance floor and beat the runner-up by a decisive margin. Multi-photo enrolment averages each identity — hardening the gallery against the slow drift of time.

Private by architecture

The model is embedded in the page. The gallery is local. The camera stays behind a passkey-gated access lock. Nothing leaves the machine.

Network-proof

The drone's own Wi-Fi hotspot has no internet behind it. Iris Mode was designed for exactly that — it never needs to reach out to work.
